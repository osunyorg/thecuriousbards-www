XVIIIth century, Norway

body: >-


Four strings under the bow and four or five more that ring in response. Resonance is at the heart of the hardingfele's sound! Known in English as the Hardanger fiddle, this iconic Norwegian instrument looks much like a violin, but a second set of strings runs beneath its fingerboard. These sympathetic strings pass through an opening in the bridge. The bow never touches them. Instead, they vibrate in response to the notes being played, enriching the sound and allowing it to linger.


The Hardanger fiddle's design allows several voices to intertwine. The flatter curve of its bridge makes it easier to bow two strings at once, so the melody can unfold over a drone, a sustained or repeated note that supports it. The sympathetic strings resonate with both melody and drone, giving the instrument its characteristic depth of sound.


The instrument also catches the eye. A carved head, often that of a lion, may take the place of the violin's scroll. Mother-of-pearl inlays and black patterns drawn in pen and ink often adorn the fingerboard and body. The Hardanger fiddle is as distinctive in its appearance as it is in its sound.


The Hardanger fiddle's history dates back to the 17th century. The earliest known example, the Jaastad fiddle, bears the date 1651. In the 18th century, fiddle maker Isak Nielsen Botnen (1669-1759) and his son Trond Isaksen Flatebø helped reshape the instrument's design and introduce it beyond its home region. Their work marked a turning point in a tradition that is still alive today.


Norwegian fiddle maker Ottar Kåsa built the ensemble's Hardanger fiddle in the style of Trond Botnen. Its slightly larger body and longer vibrating string length make it well suited to a lower tuning. Kåsa used Norwegian materials throughout. The soundboard is made of spruce grown at high altitude in Tinn, in Telemark, and the back and ribs are made of black alder from western Norway. The neck is made of aged maple. The fingerboard and tailpiece are also maple, decorated with bone and goat horn.
