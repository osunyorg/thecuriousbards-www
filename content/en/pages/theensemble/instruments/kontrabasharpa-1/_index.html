XVIIIth century, Sweden

body: >-


With a bow in one hand and a set of keys under the other, the kontrabasharpa brings together two ways of playing rarely found on a single instrument! It belongs to the nyckelharpa family of Swedish keyed fiddles, whose earliest depictions date back to the 14th century. Its key mechanism is similar to that of a hurdy-gurdy, but on the kontrabasharpa it is the bow that sets the strings vibrating.


The player's fingers therefore shorten the strings indirectly. On a violin, the fingers press directly on the strings. On the kontrabasharpa, they press keys fitted with small wooden pegs called tangents . These press against the strings, changing their vibrating length and thus the pitch of each note. The kontrabasharpa has a single row of keys, some of which act on both melody strings at once. Modern nyckelharpas generally have three rows of keys, sometimes four, allowing the player to control each melody string independently.


The name might bring a double bass to mind, but it refers to the layout of the strings rather than the instrument's range. The two melody strings lie on either side of a drone string known as the "bass". The drone provides a sustained note beneath the melody, while a set of sympathetic strings, usually about ten, adds resonance. These strings lie beyond the reach of the bow and vibrate in response to the notes being played.


Played throughout the 18th century and into the first half of the 19th, the kontrabasharpa marks a chapter in the history of Swedish keyed fiddles. Its two melody strings share a single set of keys. This arrangement, together with the drones and sympathetic strings, gives the instrument its distinctive sound.


Instrument maker Jean-Claude Condi built the ensemble's kontrabasharpa after an instrument in the collection of the Musée de la musique in Paris (catalogue no. E.1609). The museum acquired the original in 1900. It had previously belonged to the family of piano maker Hugues-Amédée Thibout, who exhibited it at the 1889 and 1900 world's fairs in Paris.
