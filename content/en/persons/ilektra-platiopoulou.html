Mezzo-soprano

body: >-


From a Monteverdi aria to a Scottish song, from a Rossini character to a Scandinavian legend, Ilektra Platiopoulou enjoys a change of scene.The Greek mezzo-soprano is drawn to the places where musical traditions meet, to the music of different languages and to stories that come alive on stage. Words and the theatrical qualities of music are central to her singing. She wants listeners to hear every word, to see each character come to life and to discover the story behind each song. Originally from Thessaloniki, she began studying singing with V. Tsampali before joining the Schola Cantorum Basiliensis, where she studied with Rosa Dominguez and Andreas Scholl. After completing a master's degree in early music in 2009, she went on to study operatic repertoire with Marcel Boone at the Musik-Akademie Basel. Her training in both early music and opera continues to inform a career that also embraces folk traditions. Her stage roles include Virtù in Monteverdi's L'incoronazione di Poppea at the Greek National Opera in Athens and the Child in Ravel's L'Enfant et les sortilèges in Basel. She has also sung two Rossini heroines, Lucilla in La scala di seta at the Académie du Festival d'Aix-en-Provence and Marianna in Il signor Bruschino at the Gulbenkian Foundation in Lisbon. In 2018, she explored the deeply moving intimacy of Poulenc's La Voix humaine with the Thessaloniki State Symphony Orchestra, in collaboration with the National Theatre of Greece. She also appears in recital, including with pianist Alphonse Cemin. With Ensemble Correspondances and Sébastien Daucé, she sang Giunone in Le Ballet royal de la nuit , a production that took her to stages across Europe, including Madrid's Teatro Real in 2022. That same year, she stepped into a very different musical world, performing Xenakis's Akanthos with dissonArt ensemble under Johannes Kalitzke at the Greek National Opera. Popular song is another of her great loves. The songs of her native Greece are especially close to her heart, particularly those of Theodorakis and Hadjidakis. Since 2016, she has found kindred spirits in The Curious Bards. Together they have explored the music of Ireland and Scotland, then Sweden and Norway, discovering repertoires rich in humour, poetry, melancholy and a love of celebration. Their rapport can be heard on three albums released by Harmonia Mundi, Extradition (2017), Indiscretion (2023) and Sublimation (2025). The collaboration continues with Rebellion! , due for release on Harmonia Mundi in January 2027, which explores Purcell's music and the popular traditions that inspired him. Its tenderness, boldness and mischief give her love of theatre full rein.
