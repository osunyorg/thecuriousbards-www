From Gaelic music to the baroque repertoire of Latin America, Louis Capeille is happy to follow wherever his harp leads him. His love of traditional music dates back to childhood. Ever since, the pleasure of discovery has shaped his work with historical instruments and basso continuo. Louis began studying the harp with Véronique Musson-Gonneau and discovered the baroque harp through Marion Fourquier. From 2006 to 2012, he studied historical harps and basso continuo at the Schola Cantorum Basiliensis with Heidrun Rosenzweig, Hopkinson Smith and Jesper Christensen. He earned a bachelor's degree followed by a master's in performance with distinction, then continued his studies at ESMUC in Barcelona with Mara Galassi and Xavier Díaz-Latorre. In 2019, he earned a further master's degree, this time in music education, with distinction at the Schola Cantorum Basiliensis. Working with musicians including Eugène Ferré, Gabriel Garrido, Eduardo Egüez, René Jacobs, Andrew Lawrence-King and Jean Tubéry has helped shape his playing. As a soloist and continuo player, he has performed internationally with ensembles including Hespèrion XXI, Elyma, A Corte Musical, Musica Fiorita, La Tempête, La Chacana, Agamemnon and La Boz Galana. In concert and on record, he has worked under the direction of Jordi Savall, Gabriel Garrido, René Jacobs, Andrew Lawrence-King, Dominique Vellard and Pierre Hamon. With The Curious Bards, Louis returns to one of his earliest musical loves, the Gaelic traditions of Ireland and Scotland. Here he brings his knowledge of historical harps and continuo playing to the traditional melodies and dances he loves. Moving between accompaniment and solo lines, he explores the many colours of the harp, always listening for a phrase to answer or a rhythm to carry forward.
