body: >-


For Bruno Harlé, a passion for the flute has never been confined to a single instrument. Whether baroque or traditional, each flute opens up a different sound world and offers its own way of shaping a phrase and telling a story through music. A child psychiatrist as well as a musician, he brings dedication to both professions, and music holds a central place in his life. He began studying the flute in the Paris region with Thierry Boiteux and Nels Lindeblad. Drawn to the sound and expressive possibilities of period instruments, he later took up the baroque flute and joined Pierre Séchet's class at the CNSMD de Paris, where he earned an advanced diploma in early music. Those years broadened his horizons. He explored the flutes and repertoire of the 19th century and discovered traditional Irish music through François Lazarevitch at the Association irlandaise de Paris. He also performed with La Grande Écurie under Jean-Claude Malgoire. Classical repertoire and folk traditions alike gave him plenty to explore. His love of dance and of playing with others led him to join arSkolpenn, with whom he plays Breton and Irish music. He deepened his knowledge of Irish music with Kevin Crawford, Brian Finnegan and Cormac Breatnach, exploring its distinctive phrasing, ornamentation and rhythmic lift. With The Curious Bards, Bruno brings these strands of his musical life together. His early music training and his experience of playing traditional music inform one another. What he enjoys most in both is finding just the right sound, setting a melody in motion and sharing a passion as strong as ever.
